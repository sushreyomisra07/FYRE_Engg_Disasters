In this activity you will transform the acceleration record into velocity and then into displacement — one integration at a time. Each integration is simply calculating the area under the curve.
Step 1 — Acceleration a(t)
g
v(t)  =  ∫₀ᵗ a(τ) dτ
The velocity at any moment equals the total area under the acceleration curve up to that point.
It tells you how fast the ground is moving — not how much it has shaken.
Step 2 — Velocity v(t)
cm/s
d(t)  =  ∫₀ᵗ v(τ) dτ
The displacement shows how far the ground actually moved from its original position.
A large displacement means the ground shifted significantly — even if the shaking felt brief.

Summary — Three Measures of Ground Motion
—
PGA (g) — Acceleration
—
PGV (cm/s) — Velocity
—
PGD (cm) — Displacement
PGA tells you about the maximum force applied to stiff structures. PGV is often a better indicator of damage to medium-height buildings. PGD matters most for long flexible structures and anything resting directly on the ground surface — like bridges or pipelines. Seismic engineers use all three depending on the structure they are designing.
